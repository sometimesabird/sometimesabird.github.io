01c · Soft split, ported / the title link

Four underlines for the title link.

Today every link shares one hover rule: the browser's default underline, which at the title's two-rem serif comes out two to three pixels thick, sits well below the baseline, and splits around the descender of Toxicity, all in the red at full strength. The same rule gives the small links a line that is heavy for their size.

B was chosen on 16 October 2026 and is now the stylesheet's rule for every text link on the homepage and the research page. Four answers below, each a complete system for the page. The left copy is live, so hover it; the right copy is frozen in its hover state, with the card rule brightened as it would be with the pointer on the card.

AHairline

The underline stays an underline, set the way a typographer would set it: one pixel, tucked close to the baseline, in the bright red at part strength so a thin line still reads on the dark ground. Skip-ink stays on; at one pixel the gap around a descender is a detail rather than a break.

Small links: the same rule, so the whole page has one underline. Trade-off: the least change, and still a line in a design that otherwise has none.

BEngravedChosen · in neu-studies.css

The underline becomes part of the surface. Titles carry a faint groove at rest: a dark cut with the lamp catching its lower lip, the same two-tone line that already divides the publications. So the title says it is a link before anyone hovers. Under the pointer, the dim red seeps into the groove as ink, matte rather than bright. The lines are painted behind the glyphs, so descenders sit over the cut instead of breaking it.

Small links: not engraved at rest; groove and ink arrive together on hover. Trade-off: the only proposal that changes the resting page, by one very quiet line under each title.

CUnderglow

No line at all. The red is light, not ink: a soft pool of it gathers under each line of the title, brightest at the centre and fading toward the ends, the way the card's rule reflects down into the card on hover. It is painted behind the glyphs, so the letters sit over it. Each link glows in its own hue: red under the cream titles, blue under the blue coauthor names.

Small links: the same glow, scaled to their size. Trade-off: the softest of the four and the most on-theme, but a glow is a vaguer signal than a line, and it needs a modern browser to fade in (it still appears, just instantly, in older ones).

DDot

The title drops the underline and borrows the nav's marker: the small dot that sits grey beside every nav item and turns red beside the current page. Here it trails the last word of a title, grey at rest, red and lit under the pointer. It is a glyph after a no-break space, so it can never wrap onto a line of its own.

Small links: paper titles get the same dot, smaller; prose links keep a hairline, because a trailing dot in a sentence reads as punctuation. Trade-off: ties the whole page to one indicator vocabulary, at the cost of one extra mark after each title.
